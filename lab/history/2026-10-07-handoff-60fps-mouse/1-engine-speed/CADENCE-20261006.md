# Cadence: why the native client judders at any frame rate (2026-10-06)

Status: measured on the Linux seat during the night of 2026-10-05 to 06, after the cleaned
`d1port` tree built and reached the world. This records what was measured, what was ruled out,
the instruments that now exist, and the open question. Numbers are [O] unless tagged.

## 1. The symptom

The operator: the gun, characters and effects move in steps; "feels like 15 fps but not all of
it"; the same at 60 FPS capped and at 300-350 FPS uncapped (clock manifest: fixed-clock override
off, outer deadline off); the same on a different monitor; the same in orbit. The step witness
read 60.0 Havok entries a second and 1.000 simulated s per wall s throughout.

## 3. What was measured

### 3.1 Frame-pair probe (`native/tools/frame_pairs_probe.py`)

Captures consecutive presented frames through the renderer's frame dump and compares neighbours
per screen region, counting only deltas above the game's per-frame dither. In orbit (camera
panning continuously, nothing else moving), uncapped at 300 FPS: the picture advanced on two
captures 8-10 ms apart and then held, unchanged in every tile, across a 25-33 ms gap, repeating.
The capture rhythm itself (two fast, one slow) is the dump mechanism's; the content result stands:
the picture advances about 20 times a second in bursts.

### 3.2 Step-gap histogram (new line in the step witness)

`Step gaps (wall ms between Havok entries): under 4: N, 4-12: N, 12-20: N, 20-28: N, 28-40: N,
over 40: N, longest X ms`, printed with each Frame stats window (`d1port/src/probe.c` hook_handler
site 6, `runtime_kernel.c` runtime_wait_report). Regular ticks at 60 a second all land in 12-20.

Uncapped (clock manifest, 480 Hz vblank, immediate presents), per 5 s window (300 ticks):

| Scene | under 4 | 4-12 | 12-20 | 20-28 | 28-40 | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| orbit / menus, 630 FPS | 93 | 7 | 100 | 0 | 100 | a triplet: stall ~33 ms, two ticks almost together, one regular tick |
| orbit, 300 FPS | 78 | 41 | 80 | 24 | 76 | same |
| world, 270 FPS, one window | 0 | 2 | 288 | 8 | 1 | regular |
| world, 270 FPS, next window | 0 | 85 | 118 | 97 | 0 | pairs: two ticks ~8 ms apart, then ~25 ms |
| world, 270 FPS, later | 15 | 61 | 146 | 53 | 24 | mixed |

The world alternates between a clean regime and a paired regime from one 5 s window to the next.

### 3.3 Where the threads are (eu-stack samples, orbit)

The main thread is `TIME_CONTROL`: in 25 of 25 samples it is inside the outer loop (eboot
0x593391) waiting on a timed semaphore (site 0xf52ae, asked 1.5-2.0 ms, signalled after ~0.3 ms
on average) for the frame's jobs. Four `job #n/4` threads do the frame; one was caught in the
GPU-completion label poll (0x7db7c3). Four `async_job` threads, `ASYNC_0`, `BLOCK_DETECT` (5 ms
sleeps), `gpu_exception_w` (500 ms sleeps), audio, voice, HTTP and file threads are idle. The
renderer's command thread `shadPS4:GpuComm` is the busiest thread in the process; in orbit it is
saturated (10-11 us a draw at 260-320 draws a frame, idle 0.3%, "worst frame 22 ms"), with stage A
waiting on stage B 68% and stage B 90% busy; the per-draw cost there is the constant-engine load
(122k dumps/s, 14.6k waits/s, 11k drains/s that waited), six times the world's 1.8 us.

perf (2 s, 2 kHz): the recording thread `bb:VkRecorder` takes 17.5% of samples; on GpuComm the
top symbols are DrainDrawPipeTo 2.5%, PostDraw 1.5%, ProcessGraphics 1.5%, StageSpecialization
1.1%, ProcessCeUpdate 1.0%.

Frame pacing in orbit uncapped: median 2.2 ms, stddev 4.0 ms, p99 18.6 ms, about 110 frames of
1500 over 1.5x the median; the command thread blocked on GPU ticks 24% in one window and 0.5% in
another. Submit gate (toggle bit 61) closed live: no change in pacing or in the probe.

## 4. Resolution (06:30-06:40 UTC)

The operator asked for a run with none of our additions: the launcher's 60 Hz manifest alone (no
cadence manifest, no clock manifest), no engine hooks, the play presentation. It looked better at
once. The A/B that followed (hooks back on, nothing else changed) was worse at once, and the
numbers agree: the draw hooks (sites 0 to 5 of patch group 18, three to four trampoline calls per
draw on the game's job threads, 30-40k calls a frame in the world) cost about 5 ms a frame. With
one tick per frame that is 45 ticks a second and a stepping picture; the uncapped runs turned the
same cost into bunched ticks. The hooks are opt-in since this night (`D1_ENGINE_HOOKS=1`; the
step witness alone, 120 calls a second, with `D1_STEP_WITNESS=1`).

The cadence manifest (the `0x5938eb` deadline byte) was called a no-op here on 2026-10-06 04:00
because the Linux baseline without it paced at 16.65 ms under the 480 Hz presentation. That was
wrong as a general statement: on Windows under the stock 60 Hz vblank (08:40-08:55 UTC), the 60 Hz
manifest alone gave a locked 60 presents a second with the operator's character moving at about
half speed, and adding the byte (14 writes) gave the same locked 60 at the right speed ("feels
right"). The byte stays in every play configuration, as in the emulator's profile; why the Linux
run paced at 16.65 ms without it is open (the step witness on each host settles it). The
emulator's play profile does not arm its wall-credit gate (observe mode only), so with the byte
the native play configuration and the emulator's game configuration are the same. The reading below was written before the A/B and is kept as the
record of what the instruments showed with the hooks installed; its two regimes were the hooks'
cost seen two ways.

## 4a. Reading before the resolution (with the hooks installed)

The simulation's ticks are delivered irregularly by the game's own outer loop, and the picture
follows the ticks. Two regimes:

- Light scenes: the outer loop stalls about 33 ms once per three ticks, then runs two ticks at
  once (continuation frames). The stall is not a sleep (no sleep site of that length on the main
  thread), not the NP thread, not power management. The main thread waits for the frame's jobs;
  what holds the jobs for 33 ms is not yet shown. The renderer is saturated in these scenes
  (constant-engine load), and the game, whose own frames are nearly free there, runs ahead of it;
  the stall length is consistent with a queue draining or a periodic expensive frame on the
  command thread ("worst frame 22 ms" in a 3 ms scene), not with steady slowness.
- World: the outer frame cost sits at the 16.7 ms budget (10k draws at 1.8 us on the command
  thread plus the game's own jobs), so the loop alternates between one tick per frame and two
  ticks per 33 ms frame. Without render interpolation both regimes read as steps on everything
  that moves per tick (gun, characters, effects), while per-frame camera motion stays smooth.

This is why no cap, clock, display or cache change moved it, and why 348 FPS felt like 60.

## 4b. Play configuration verdict (06:53 UTC)

Uncapped without the hooks: the launcher's 60 Hz manifest plus `guest/CUSA00219-native-clock.xml`
(fixed-clock override off, outer deadline off), box-walk presentation defaults (480 Hz virtual
vblank, immediate presents, Mailbox), `D1_ENGINE_HOOKS` unset. The operator, landing in
Restoration and playing: "feels better now, even when it goes under 60". In the heavy view they
were in: 13.4k draws a frame at 1.64-1.71 us on the command thread, 43-45 FPS, pacing median
21.4-22.6 ms with stddev 2.5-3.2 ms; the simulation stays at 60 ticks a second (a frame under
60 carries two ticks) so the game's speed is exact. The capped play configuration (override on,
one tick per frame) runs the game at frame rate divided by 60 in such views, which is why it felt
slow there. This is the play configuration until the per-draw cost comes down. Correction later the same
morning (section 4): the cadence manifest is not a no-op; keep it in. Linux play configuration:
the 60 Hz manifest, the cadence manifest and the clock manifest. Windows play configuration as
first played (08:52 UTC): the 60 Hz manifest and the cadence manifest on the stock 60 Hz vblank, a
locked 60 at the right speed; the clock manifest and the 480 Hz presentation are the uncapped
measurement set there as on Linux.

## 5. What remains, and the next measurements

- Re-measure everything in FRAME-CAPS-REMOVED-20261005.md without the hooks (its section 9).
- "Not perfect": in the world the baseline paces at 16.5 ms median with stddev 2.6-2.7 ms and
  p99 24 ms. Those are frames over budget on the renderer's command thread (about 10k draws at
  1.8 us); the per-draw cost work in PLAN-100FPS-20261005.md is the lever, and the uncapped
  configuration without hooks tells how much headroom there is.
- The orbit triplet (33 ms stall, two ticks together) was measured only with the hooks installed.
  One run with `D1_STEP_WITNESS=1` alone (no draw hooks) settles whether it exists at all without
  them; if it does, the per-frame timeline described earlier (outer-loop entry, ticks that frame,
  label-poll duration) is the instrument.
- The sim and the display: with the game at 60 ticks a second and presents immediate on a 480 Hz
  virtual vblank, the 60 Hz display samples whatever frame is latest; a frame-time jitter of a
  few ms around the refresh shows as an occasional repeat. Present-aligned pacing (one frame per
  real refresh, frame ready before it) is the console design and needs the renderer under budget;
  render interpolation is the path above 60.
- Instruments to keep off unless measuring: the draw hooks. Instruments that are free: the step
  witness and its gap histogram, the frame-pair probe.
- bbport's draw-preparation workers (toggle bit 13), tried live at 06:41 UTC in the Restoration
  vista with the V# check in place: 19.2k prepared pipelines used against 3.16M not (26.5k of
  those rejected for vertex V#s that differed), a 0.6% hit rate; command thread 1.51 against
  1.47 us a draw, no change in FPS or pacing, picture clean. As predicted on 2026-10-05: the
  workers read before this submission's constant dumps land, so they rarely match in motion. Off
  by default stays right; no gain to find there.
- Without the hooks, in the chain's vista (10.7k draws a frame): 60 FPS, command thread 1.45-1.51
  us a draw, idle 2-6%, pacing median 16.3 ms, stddev 2.2-2.7 ms, p99 23-25 ms. The frame fits
  the budget with almost nothing to spare; heavier views do not.
