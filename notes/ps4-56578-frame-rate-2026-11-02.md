# PS4 56578: frame-rate presets (2 November 2026)

The goal (the user's, 2 Nov): frame-rate presets (30, 60, 100, 120, 144, 165, 240, uncapped) and any
resolution up to 4K, ending as options in the game's own settings menu. This note covers the frame
rate's first step: fixed presets that work in game.

## How the game paces frames

- The 60 Hz patch (`ps4/patches/CUSA00219-56578-lab-orbit-60hz.xml`) sets the engine's time control
  at `0x592A03..0x592A21`: period 1001/60 ms, rate 60, step 1000/60 ms.
- Read live with `tools/ps4_setup/d1-frame-timing.py`, the presentation cadence table (behind the
  pointer at `0x26BCD08`) is built from them. `+0x38` is the rate and `+0x08` the period in
  microseconds, and the engine's game-time clock (`*(0x26BC880)`) ticks at the rate.
- The table's `+0x28` stays 16,683 us at every rate. The 60 Hz patch's "outer cadence" entry points the
  present deadline at it (`0x594A3B`). That is the same as the original `+0x08` read at 60 and a
  60 fps cap at any other rate, so above 60 it must be off.

## Presets

`tools/ps4_setup/d1-fps-patch.py N` writes the patch for rate N from the 60 Hz one. It sets period
1001/N ms, rate N and step 1000/N ms, and disables the outer cadence entry for N != 60; at 60 the
output is byte-identical to the original. The emulator must also present that fast:
`GPU.vblank_frequency >= N` in `user/config.json` (edited byte-exact) and `D1_FLIP_RATE=0`.

Measured in orbit (lab session, geared Guardian, emulator FPS counter on):

| Preset | Table period | Engine | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 16,683 us | 60.0 ticks/s | 60 FPS (16.7 ms) |
| 120 | 8,341 us | 120.0 ticks/s | 120 FPS (8.3 ms) |
| 240 | 4,170 us | 239.9 ticks/s | 194 FPS (5.1 ms), rendering-bound on this PC |

The engine's period matches its tick rate at each preset (8.3 ms × 120), so game time runs at real
speed.

## In the mission, and switching live

In the mission's opening at 120 (`d1-agr-run.ps1` with the 120 Hz patch, vblank 120), the screen
showed about 41 FPS (this PC) and the engine ticked 80-85 times a second: **about 0.7x real
time**, and the loading sat much longer than at 60.
- The limit is how many ticks a second the CPU can simulate, not a number per frame. In the mission this
  PC simulates about 70; in orbit, where a tick is cheap, it ran 990 (five per frame at 192 FPS). An
  earlier reading of "two ticks per frame" was a coincidence of the mission's numbers.
- A preset above what the PC can render is slow motion, not smoother. The settings need a guard:
  the rate must follow what the screen can hold.

**The rate can be changed while the game runs.** Its live state is five values (the fourth found when an
earlier version changed only three, and the engine ticked 99 times a second at 1/86 s each, 1.15x real
time; the fifth on 3 November, when a rifle kept the start-up rate's shot interval, see Fidelity):
- the cadence table's `+0x08` (period in us, 1001000/N) and `+0x38` (rate);
- the table's `+0x40`, the tick step in us (1000000/N), which the scheduler ticks on;
- the clock's `+0x08` (tick in s, 1/N);
- the clock's `+0x04` (ticks a second, N), which the game turns seconds into ticks with.

Game time per second is ticks/s times the clock's tick, and 1.0 is real.

`tools/ps4_setup/d1-frame-rate.py N` writes them and measures the result. In the running mission it
switched 120 → 60 (60.0 ticks/s, 1.0x; the stalled loading screen then came through), 60 → 120
(85 ticks/s, 0.7x) and back (60.3, 1.0x), with no restart. That is the mechanism the settings menu
needs. A limiter can use it too, lowering the rate to what the screen can hold.

## The emulator holds the rate: D1_FPS

The project's shadPS4 fork (branch `d1-frame-rate`; deployed build 2f69d66) adds `D1_FPS`:
- **`D1_FPS=N` (10..1000), a preset.** Once the game has set up its timing, the emulator writes the
  five values for N and keeps them, rewriting them if a loading transition resets the clock.
- **The guard.** Each second it measures game time (ticks × tick length). If that falls below 0.97 of
  real time for two seconds, the rate steps down to what the engine achieves, never below 60. After
  30 s of real time it climbs back toward N, stopping 10 below the lowest rate that failed in the last
  5 minutes.
- **`D1_FPS=0`, uncapped.** One tick per presented frame, as PC games mean it, not as many ticks as the
  CPU can run. A flip counter in the presenter gives the frame rate. Every 5 s a rate the screen keeps
  up with probes 20% higher; a screen below 80% of the rate brings it down, and that rate is not
  probed again for 2 minutes. It starts at 60.
- **Presentation.** The presenter's vblank runs at the target rate (500 Hz uncapped), and
  `D1_FLIP_RATE=0` flips on every vblank. Boot with a generated patch above 60 (`d1-fps-patch.py`), so
  the outer cadence entry is off.

Measured on the lab PC:

| Setting | Where | Settled at | Engine | Screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1_FPS=120 | orbit | 120 | 120.0 ticks/s | 120 FPS |
| D1_FPS=120 | mission opening | 60 (120 → 70 → 60) | 59.7-60.0 ticks/s, real time | ~40 FPS |
| D1_FPS=0 | orbit | 211 (climbs from 60 in 5 s steps) | 210.7 ticks/s | ~193 FPS |

The earlier guard versions are worth a line each:
- with only three of the four values written it ran at 1.15x real time;
- judging while the clock stands still in loading dropped the rate to 60 at once;
- without the ceiling it climbed and fell every 30 s;
- uncapped first held 990 ticks for 192 frames, then flapped between 190 and 273.

## Fidelity

D1 on PS4 simulated at 30 Hz; the 60 Hz patch doubles that, and these presets go further. Uncapped
holds about one tick per frame so it doesn't run the simulation far from the screen's rate. Three
checks, with the rate switched live (`tools/ps4_setup/d1_rate.py`) in sessions fps-jump-a (booted
at 120) and fps-fire-60 (booted at 30, the repo's default lab patch, which has no 60 Hz time control).

### The clock's ticks a second (3 November)

The engine clock keeps the rate twice: +0x08 the tick period (1/N, float) and **+0x04 the ticks a
second (N, integer)**. The clock's set-up (0x591710) writes both from the time control the 60 Hz
patch edits. The helpers at 0x591BF0-0x591C80 turn seconds into ticks with +0x04 (0x591C60: seconds
x [+4]; 0x591C40: n x [+4]; 0x591C00: a ceiling division by it); 0x591C80 alone has 229 call sites in
162 functions. The live switch wrote only +0x08 until 3 November, so everything timed through those
helpers stayed on the start-up rate's ticks.

A third value beside them, the float at [0x26BC888]+0x04 (1.0 in a normal start), paces the engine:
written as 30/N it halved the ticks at 60 (30 ticks a second, half real time). It stays as it is.
At 1/150 it is a slow motion of one tick every 5 seconds, useful for inspecting a tick at a time.

### Rate of fire

`tools/ps4_setup/d1-fire-probe.py`: the starter Khvostov's magazine count (found with
`tools/ps4_setup/d1-memvalue.py`: 25, then 24 and 23 after single shots) sampled every 1 ms while fire
is held 1.5 s of game time; the interval between shots from the engine's tick count.

| Switch | 30 | 60 | 120 | 240 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Booted at 30, no switch | 4 ticks, 133 ms, **450 rounds/min** | | | |
| Booted at 120, +0x08 only | 16 ticks, 112/min | 16 ticks, 225/min | 16 ticks, 450/min | 16 ticks, 900/min |
| Booted at 30, +0x08 only | | 4 ticks, 900/min | 4 ticks, 1155/min | |
| Booted at 30, with +0x04 | 4 ticks, 450/min | 8 ticks, 450/min | 16 ticks, 450/min | 32 ticks, 450/min |

The rifle fires at 450 rounds a minute when the game starts at any rate (4 ticks at 30, 16 at 120).
The shot interval is converted per shot, so writing +0x04 keeps it at 450 at every rate.

### The jump

`tools/ps4_setup/d1-jump-probe.py`: jump (NumPad2) held a given time of game time, the Guardian's
unit position sampled every 2 ms; peak height above the standing height, airtime, sideways drift.
With +0x04 written, booted at 30, on flat ground by the Breach rifle (drift 0.00 m), one jump each
(repeats agree to the millimetre):

| Hold | 30 (PS4) | 60 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 ms | 1.268 m | 1.222 m | |
| 40 ms | 1.268 m | 1.222 m | 1.519 m |
| 60 ms | 1.268 m | 1.344 m | |
| 80 ms | 1.268 m | 1.532 m | |
| 100 ms | 1.389 m | 1.601 m | |
| 120 ms | 1.389 m | 1.655 m | 1.79 m |
| 160 ms | 1.563 m | 1.726 m | |
| 200 ms | 1.621 m | 1.765 m | |
| 300 ms | 1.722 m | 1.781 m | |
| 400 ms | 1.750 m | 1.781 m | 1.797 m |
| 1,000 ms | 1.754 m | 1.780 m | 1.797 m |

Two effects:
- **A full hold** peaks 1.7% higher at 60 and 2.6% at 120 than at 30, airtime 1.033 s against
  1.050: the integration error of a fixed step, converging as the step shrinks (session fps-jump-a
  measured the same: 1.760, 1.795, 1.826 and 1.839 m at 30, 60, 120 and 240).
- **A partial hold is counted in ticks.** The curve at 60 is the curve at 30 at twice the hold (60
  ms at 60: 1.344 m, 120 ms at 30: 1.389; 100 and 200 ms: 1.601 and 1.621; 160 and 320 ms: 1.726 and
  about 1.73), with a dead zone of about 2.5 ticks at both (80 ms at 30, 40 ms at 60). A typical press
  of 100-200 ms jumps 15-19% higher at 60 than on PS4. This is in the 60 Hz patch itself, not only
  the presets: whatever boosts the jump while it is held works per tick, not per second. Writing
  +0x04 doesn't change it. Not found yet: the hold state is neither in the unit object's first
  0x4000 bytes nor within 64 KB before or 256 KB after it (the button packet at unit+0x130 is copied
  to +0xA64 and +0x80 each tick by 0xBD22F0).
