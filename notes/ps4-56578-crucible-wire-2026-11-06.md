# PS4 56578: Crucible matches driven on the wire (6 November 2026)

`pvp.rs` (#313) had the Crucible match: lobby, countdown, play, rounds, sudden death, mercy,
forfeits and results, a scoreboard shaped like BullShcript's, and the Trials and Iron Banner rules.
Nothing drove it from what the clients send. This change drives it from the clients' own reports
and serves it on each client's udt 5.

**Offline only.** It was built and unit-tested with fake clients. No game, lab or emulator run
was made, and nothing here is claimed in play. Every hook is off by default: with `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE`
unset the host serves exactly as before.

## What was built

All of it is in `d1-activity-host`, in three new modules and marked hooks:

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| [pvp_driver.rs](../ps4/d1-server/crates/d1-activity-host/src/pvp_driver.rs) | `MatchDriver`: one match per instance (activity host id), fed by the clients' incident reports, publishing the lifetime and the scoreboard in order, granting at the results. `MatchRegistry`: every instance's driver, shared by its connections |
| [pvp_wire.rs](../ps4/d1-server/crates/d1-activity-host/src/pvp_wire.rs) | `CrucibleConn`: one per connection. It feeds the driver what its client reports and sends that client what the driver publishes, through the script bridge's row table and the host's wire codec |
| [scoreboard_rows.rs](../ps4/d1-server/crates/d1-activity-host/src/scoreboard_rows.rs) | The whole kind-15 scoreboard image (`0x808008D0`): every player, every team, the global block and the notifications. Also the lifetime value's named-value list, where `score_to_win` can ride |

The hooks, each marked `stream R`:

- `state.rs`: one field, a setter, the message hook and the udt 5 hook. The udt 5 hook takes the
  connection's stream only while it serves a Crucible map;
- `main.rs`: the configuration, next to the script bridge's;
- `listener.rs`: shared instances turn on under `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE`, so the physics host is elected;
- `rewards.rs`: `ActivityOutcome::Crucible`;
- `reward_forward.rs`: the outcome's log word and its `rep=` reputation tokens;
- `d1-responder/src/rewards/remote.rs`: the `grant` line's `rep=NAME:N` tokens. They are added to
  the character the grant names, after its rewards, under the same checks.

## What drives what

```text
stream H MatchFormed ─(crucible-match line, or the table's playlist=)─> MatchRegistry::join
client join_request ──> CrucibleConn ──> MatchDriver::join (team by fireteam, physics host noted)
client udt 18 ──(index → name hash, 56578 catalogue)──> classify ──> pair kill/death ──> pvp.rs
pvp.rs effects ──> Publish::{Lifetime, Scoreboard} ──> every CrucibleConn ──> RowTable ──> udt 5
results (6) ──> ActivityRewards::grant_activity_rewards, once per present player
```

### Starting a match

A match starts when a Crucible map's instance forms. The driver needs to know the playlist, so it
looks in three places, in this order:

1. **Stream H's `MatchFormed`.** Its `match_id` is the instance's host id. It names the playlist,
   the map, the player count and the fireteams. The matchmaker runs in the responder, another
   process, so a formed match crosses over as a line in `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_MATCHES`:

   ```text
   crucible-match HOST PLAYLIST MAP PLAYERS [FIRETEAM,...]
   ```

   The responder does not write these lines yet. `MatchSetup::from_formed` is the in-process form.
2. **The activity table line's `playlist=`.**
3. **`D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_PLAYLIST`.**

A map with none of these starts no match and is served as before.

The match waits in its lobby (lifetime 1) until `MatchFormed::players` have joined, or two
without a formed match. Then the countdown starts.

### What the clients report, and how it is read

The PvE captures show how a client reports combat. A capture of The Nexus, read locally, was
decoded with the 56578 codec, and its incident ids were mapped through the 51539 → 56578 table.

- Every kill the client makes goes out as one udt 18. The chain starts with `any_kill` (734) and
  `kill` (1825), followed by qualifier incidents, all body selector 1, with one metadata body of
  about 192-216 bytes.
- The client's deaths go out as `player_died` (2079), its revives as `player_resurrected` (3615).

The 56578 catalogue also names the PvP incidents:

- `suicide` (643) and `betrayal` (1435);
- `assist_damage` (2695);
- the zone captures: `pvp_dominion_zone_captured` (774), `capture` (1338) and
  `pvp_zone_captured_a/b/c` (2378/2376/2377).

A test checks each id and hash against the committed catalogue.

`classify` turns one udt 18 into reports. A chain is one event:

- a suicide or a betrayal replaces the plain kill its chain also names;
- a zone capture counts once.

**Who did it is a stand-in.** The kill body is selector 1's metadata. Read from the 56578 image:

- The worldserver kill metadata class (`c_incident_metadata_worldserver_kill_interface`, vptr
  `0x190AF20`) names its body type through slot 3. Slot 3 reads type cell `0x26AAD40`, and the
  type-cell table pairs that cell with name hash `0x3240684C`.
- The descriptor carrying that hash is `0x80800AAB`, a 176-byte body. It holds two `0x80800E70`
  participants, at `+0x18` and `+0x58`.
- A participant's `+0x28` is the activity player id, from the rifle-pickup runs.

This mirrors exactly how the 965 body was bound. Neither codec decodes a live body yet: d1-rsat
does not implement field kind `0x19`, and the client's own codec faults at `0x7201F` on the live
bodies.

So the driver credits **the reporting connection**:

- a `kill` chain is the reporter's kill;
- a `player_died` is the reporter's death;
- a kill and a death from another team within one second (stand-in) are one kill;
- a kill nobody died for still scores the kill;
- a death nobody claimed is a death with no killer.

This is right only if each console reports its own player's kills and deaths. If the physics host
reports every kill, the attribution is wrong. The evidence list below says what settles it.

**Rounds.** The clients report no round end. In Elimination and Trials, `pvp.rs`'s alive count
decides a round from the reported deaths; revives restore a player. Between rounds the lifetime
stays at 3. What the live host sent at a round boundary is open.

### The lifetime

| Value | When | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the instance's match is made (lobby, pregame) | every live activity; the spawn gate rejects 1 |
| 2 | the countdown (`D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_COUNTDOWN_LIFETIME=0` keeps 1) | BullShcript's runtime state + 1. Never served in PvE; the spawn gate rejects it and nothing else reads it. **Unverified** |
| 3 | play, every round | the spawn gate accepts 3 |
| 6 | the results. The final scoreboard goes out in the frame before it | `0xAB9B80` builds the results from the scoreboard UI store at 6; the copy into the store stops at 7 |
| 7 | 30 s after 6 (post-game) | 36 of the 39 live activities that reached 7 |
| 8 | after `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_REMATCH_MS` (stand-in 30 s) | 8 is the client's own end-of-activity transition: for a playlist, the rematch decision `0x75E600` |

As with the script bridge, each value reaches the client in its own frame. A connection that
joins late first gets the world's full list, carrying the current lifetime and scoreboard.

### The scoreboard

`scoreboard_rows::image` writes the whole `0x808008D0` image at the offsets of the 56578
descriptor; a test checks them against `rtti/fields.tsv`. The image holds:

- **One row per player** (up to 16). Each row has:
  - the 8-byte native identity: the 40-byte identity's player field. This is our reading; the
    post-game run's row carried the same kind-`0x123` identity as the cinematic rows;
  - the stat count and the stat columns.
- **One row per team** (team id, stat count, stats).
- **The global block.**
- **Up to two notifications.**
- **The category**: FNV-1 of nothing, as the post-game run sent it.

The image encodes and decodes natively (d1-rsat, with the eboot).

**The columns are content, and only three are known:** definition `0x80AA3EE6` has 23 player
stats, with `kills` at 0, `precision_kills` at 18 and `deaths` at 22. Which definition each mode
uses, the other 20 columns, and the team and global lists are not in the exported content.

- The default player columns are that list, with the unnamed slots sent as 0.
- The team score is in team slot 0, a **stand-in**.
- `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_COLUMNS` replaces both.

Every player and every team gets a row, even before it has scored.

### `score_to_win`

The client reads `score_to_win` in one place, `0x4001A0`, through the option lookup `0x118870`.
How the lookup works:

- `0x118870` gets an options object from the activity manager (`0x10C250`, vtable `+0x40`).
- `0x118540` binary-searches that object: `{i32 count; 24-byte entries {u32 name hash; typed value}}`.
- `0x4001A0` takes the entry whose type at `+4` is the int type, and its value at `+8`.

The 56578 descriptors have exactly one such list, `0x8080533D`. It sits in two replicated records:

- the lifetime value, at `+0x10`;
- the global activity state's `0x80800CC5`, at `+4` (blob `+0x64`).

Which of the two the manager returns is not read yet. The driver owns the lifetime row, so
`score_to_win` rides there.

**The int type's tag is a candidate.** The cell `0x4001A0` compares against (slot `0x1996058` →
`0x28DBD70`) is paired with name hash `0xA801403A`. No anchored descriptor carries that hash where
the 965 and kill bodies' did (anchor − `0x48`). The nearest is `0x80800007`, a 4-byte biased i32,
with the hash at anchor − `0x60`.

So the tag is configuration, `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_SCORE_TO_WIN=0x80800007`, and nothing is sent by
default. It needs `D1_RSAT_NATIVE=1`: the client's codec has no int typed value registered
(`d1_oracle::sensor::TYPED_VALUE_TYPES`).

The values are the modes' stand-ins: the score limit, or a round mode's round count.

### Rewards

At the results, every player still in the instance is granted once, through the host's one
rewards trait (`ActivityRewards`; the forwarder under `D1_REWARDS=1`). The grant is
`ActivityOutcome::Crucible`, made for the playlist's hash. It carries:

- won or lost, mercy, and the player's place;
- the faction and its reputation: `faction_pvp`, or `faction_event_iron_banner` for Iron Banner.
  The amounts are `crucible.json`'s stand-ins;
- level advantages (Iron Banner, Trials). The physics host applies them; the server records them;
- the Trials card, kept in memory per character (the player store has no card field):
  - Favor, Mercy and Boon work as `pvp.rs` has them;
  - the card ends at nine wins (flawless: the Lighthouse) or three losses;
  - the rewards come at 5, 7 and 9 wins;
  - a card that is over is replaced by a fresh one at the next match, a stand-in for buying one.

The grant line, for example:

```text
grant id:… 0x… completed source=crucible:won:ib:place=1 rep=faction_event_iron_banner:150
```

A Trials win adds `rep=r1_s4_tickets.pvp.trials_of_osiris.wins:N`, the manifest's ticket
progression (`D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_TRIALS_TICKET` sets the prefix). A player who left before the results
gets nothing, as in D1.

### The physics host

Combat is peer-to-peer and the server simulates none of it: the driver only reads reports. Under
`D1_AH_CRUCIBLE` the listener turns shared instances on, so `group.rs` (#312) elects the instance's
physics host: the first member, and on a leave the earliest who remains. The driver records who
holds it and logs each migration. A test checks two things:

- a delta carries only the match's rows (the lifetime and the scoreboard);
- the role moves to another console when its holder leaves.

## Configuration

| Knob | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE=1` | On (with shared instances). Needs `D1_AH_SENSOR_PHASE`, as the script bridge does |
| `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_ACTIVITIES` | The Crucible activity table: the script bridge's line grammar plus `playlist=HASH`. LIST may be `@generated` (`D1_AH_WORLDS`) |
| `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_PLAYLIST` | The playlist for a launch that nothing else names |
| `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_MATCHES` | Formed matches, one `crucible-match` line each, polled |
| `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_COLUMNS` | The scoreboard column table |
| `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_SCORE_TO_WIN=TAG` | Send `score_to_win` with that int type tag (with `D1_RSAT_NATIVE=1`) |
| `D1_AH_CRUCIBLE_COUNTDOWN_LIFETIME=0`, `_COUNTDOWN_MS`, `_REMATCH_MS`, `_TRIALS_TICKET` | The driver's stand-ins |

`crucible.json` is read from `D1_AH_ACTIVITY_FRAMEWORK`'s folder when it names one, else from
`ps4/fixtures/56578/activities`.

## Tests

`cargo test -p d1-activity-host`. Through fake clients (each a `CrucibleConn` with a recording
codec):

- **A whole Control match**, four clients:
  - the formed match, and fireteams kept together on two teams;
  - the first member as physics host;
  - the countdown at 2, then play at 3;
  - the consoles reporting kills and deaths, and one zone capture (200 stand-in points);
  - the score limit;
  - the final board before 6, with every player and both teams;
  - 6, 7 30 s later, then 8;
  - one grant per player, two wins.
- **A whole Elimination match**, six clients:
  - rounds decided from the reported deaths alone;
  - a revive in the first round, then a second kill;
  - first to the round count;
  - the lifetime held at 3 through the rounds;
  - six grants.
- **A Trials card**, over nine matches:
  - Boon counts the first win twice;
  - Mercy forgives the first loss;
  - nine wins, flawless, the Lighthouse, three rewards;
  - the opponent's card ends twice at three losses;
  - a fresh card after a finished one.
- **Iron Banner**: the faction's reputation (completed + win bonus), level advantages, the `rep=`
  token; a forfeit's leaver gets nothing.
- **The rest:**
  - the server relays only the match's rows, and the physics host moves;
  - `score_to_win` in the lifetime;
  - a map with no playlist starts nothing;
  - the incidents match the committed catalogue;
  - chains classify as one event;
  - kill/death pairing and flushing;
  - the `crucible-match` line and `MatchFormed`;
  - the scoreboard offsets against `fields.tsv`, and the image's contents;
  - the column files;
  - the named-value list as the client's lookup reads it;
  - with the eboot, both images through native RSAT.
- **In d1-responder**: the `rep=` grammar.

## Placeholders, and what one captured match would settle

Still stand-ins, each labelled where it is set:

- every score limit, time limit, mercy lead, round count and point value (`crucible.json`);
- the reputation amounts;
- the countdown, the round break, the pairing window and the 7 → 8 delay;
- the team score column;
- the int type tag;
- the kill attribution.

**What to record.** One private capture of a whole matchmade Crucible match would settle most of
them: Control first, then Elimination or Trials. It should be captured from a console that is not
the physics host, and ideally from two consoles in the same match. Read it per activity host with
`d1-srcp.py`. Record:

1. **Who reports a kill.** For each kill: which console's udt 18 carries the `kill` chain, and
   which the `player_died`; the chain's incident list; the selector-1 metadata bytes; and both
   participants' activity player ids at `+0x18+0x28` and `+0x58+0x28`, decoded once kind `0x19`
   decodes. This settles the attribution and the pairing window.
2. **The lifetime's values and times**, with `d1-srcp.py lifetime`: whether 2 is served for the
   countdown; how long the countdown and the round breaks last; whether 5 or 9 appear; the delay
   from 6 to 7 and from 7 to 8; and whether an Elimination round boundary changes it.
3. **The kind-15 scoreboard**:
   - each player row's identity, the stat count and which column moves on a kill, a death, an
     assist and a capture;
   - the team rows' team ids and which slot is the team score;
   - the global block, the notifications (which name hashes, when), the category at `+0x4D3C`,
     and `+0x4D40`.

   This settles the columns and the scoreboard definition for the mode.
4. **The lifetime value's named-value list and the global activity state's `+0x64` list**: whether
   `score_to_win` is in either, with which type tag and value, and the other options with it.
   This settles the int type and the mode's score limit.
5. **The final scores, the time played and the round count** at the end, against the score: the
   limits and the mercy rule.
6. **The zone capture incidents** in Control: which of 774, 1338 and 2376-2378 a console sends,
   and from whose console.
7. **The membership** (udt 11): the elected physics host, and any migration.
8. **What the host sends at the results and after**: the completion chain, if any (does a Crucible
   match send 965?), and the profile deltas (reputation, marks, the post-game drops).
