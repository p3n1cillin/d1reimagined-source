# PS4 56578: drops, BABOON and "Contacting Destiny Servers" (2 November 2026)

In the afternoon runs the player saw "Contacting Destiny Servers" at random, and was kicked out of the
mission with a BABOON error. These are two problems, with two causes.

## "Contacting Destiny Servers": the host was too quiet

The client times how long each connection has gone without a byte. Past a threshold it prints an
`investment_bap_manager:drought` report (each connection's "last … ms ago") and warns.
- In sense126's client log (up to its cap) there were 71 drought reports. Every one had the
  activity-host connection (`OUT FAH`, port 4110) idle at least 5 s: 68 at exactly 5 s, the rest at
  10-12 s. The responder connection (`OUT PRIMARY`) never tripped one on its own.
- The host's heartbeat was a quarter of the client's 20.5 s host-crash timeout: 5,125 ms, plus up to
  one 1.3 s listener poll. That kept the session alive, but every quiet stretch crossed the 5 s line.
- #230 sets the heartbeat to 2,000 ms (worst gap 2.5 s) and records the measured line as
  `CLIENT_DROUGHT_WARN_MS`, with a test that holds the margin.

### 3 November: slow replies at orbit load

The toast came back at orbit load (an orbit boot, `d1-lab-served.ps1`; frame-per-second capture with
`tools/ps4_setup/d1-toast-watch.py`), in both the stock profile (session toast-stock, 06:08:51Z, gone
within 3 s) and a new Guardian's (splash-r8). The activity host is idle there; the responder answered
every request that expects an answer.

With `D1_RESPONDER_LOG_TIME=1` (new; the responder's log is untimed by default) session toast-stock-b
timed each reply from request to send: most within 80 ms, but the first `client_to_world_server_request`
(investment 702) took **1,460 ms** and the account-id translation **910 ms**, and in both the time went
between two log lines with only trivial work in between. The same 702 later took 46 ms. The responder
was not busy: it was **starved**. At orbit load the emulator keeps every core busy, and at normal
priority the servers wait their turn; a reply that waits long enough shows the toast.

`Start-D1ContainedBoot.ps1` now starts the servers (both responders, the activity host, shadnet) at
**above-normal priority**. Session toast-stock-c: the slowest of 33 replies took **37 ms** (typical 2 ms,
before 50-75), and no frame showed the toast.

## The drops: the emulator freezes

The drops have one signature, five times in two runs (sense125 at 14:57; sense126 at 16:38, 16:47,
16:55 and 16:57):
1. the client's 2 s keepalives stop;
2. the host keeps sending;
3. about 25-30 s later the host's socket fails with `WSAECONNABORTED` (10053), the client's TCP
   having stopped acknowledging;
4. the client runs its private-activity repair: it rejoins, relaunches 164, and the host serves
   the mission from step 0.

On screen that is BABOON and the mission restarting. The client froze; the host did not. Memory was
not short: 13.5 GB free, with the client at 5.6 GB.

They began in the Breach hall's chest legs. In sense126 they then came every few minutes, even
during the revival cutscene.

**A suspect, not proven: the emulator's own log.** 90% of `shad_log.txt` was one renderer warning,
`SanitizeCopyLayers: Coercing copy source layers N and destination layers N+1`. It ran 1→32 every
frame, about 45,000 lines per 50,000 for the whole run.
- `Log.sync` is on, so every line is written synchronously.
- The filter `Render.Vulkan:Error` never matched, because the class is named `Render_Vulkan`
  (`src/common/logging/classes.cpp`).
- The log hit its 100 MB cap mid-run (`Log.size_limit`), after which nothing was logged.

The local config is now `*:Info Render_Vulkan:Error` with a 512 MB cap. The next run shows whether
the drops stop with it.

Edit `user/config.json` byte-exact: rewriting it with Python's `json.dump` (same keys and values,
other formatting) stopped this build from starting at all (sense127 never booted).

## Run sense128 (17:11), with both fixes

- **The log:** two minutes in, 7,149 Info lines and no `SanitizeCopyLayers`; 8.5 MB after 20 minutes of
  play.
- **No drop through the Breach hall's chest legs**, where every earlier attempt today dropped.
- **The heartbeat:** the host sends every 2 s, as logged.
- **Drought reports remain (30 by 17:32), and the cause is on the client.** Its activity-host received
  count stood still for up to 15 s while the host sent. The client's own traffic paused too: the host
  saw nothing from it for 12.5 s at 17:18:02, 9.1 s at 17:19:40 and 16.6 s at 17:21:39.
  - Those pauses fall where new content arrives: the Breach lights and props before the platform,
    and the hall's first waves at chest-0 and chest-1.
  - So the client's network job pauses while the game instantiates content, in both directions,
    below the 20.5 s limit now. The emulator caches translated shaders (`user/cache/CUSA00219/*.spv`),
    so the likelier load is the game's own streaming (package reads, decompression) on the emulated
    CPU. Profiling the emulator during a wave spawn is the next step.
- **The remaining log noise** (`sceNpPartyCheckCallback` stub errors, `IT_SET_PREDICATION`
  warnings) is filtered from the next boot: `Render:Error Lib.NpParty:Critical`.

- **The end: WEASEL at junction-west, 17:45:17.** Both the activity host's and the responder's TCP
  connections failed with 10053 in the same instant, and the client showed "Could not connect to
  Destiny servers … weasel" with no rejoin.
  - 10053 is this machine's own TCP stack giving up, after roughly 20-30 s of unacknowledged
    retransmissions. A peer that closes gives 10054.
  - So the emulator stopped acknowledging on every socket at once: the same 20-30 s freeze as
    before. The earlier drops only cut the activity host because the responder had no data in
    flight then.
  - #229 then took the session's first connection for a rejoin (the replay starts before the client
    connects) and waited ten minutes; #231 fixes that.

## The disk

The game's packages, the emulator, its log and cache, the staging folder and the run's recordings
are all on F:, a 2 TB spinning hard disk (Seagate ST2000DM008). The freezes come as the game
streams new content: the Breach props, each wave, junction-west. At those moments the disk is also
taking the synchronous log writes and the video recording. One spinning disk seeking between them
can hold a read for many seconds.

The test, in two steps (the user's choice):
1. **sense129** runs without the video recording (`-NoRecord`).
2. If freezes remain, copy the 50 GB game dump to the 240 GB SSD (E:, 61 GB free) and run from
   there. F: stays as the fallback.

**sense129 (no recording) still dropped**, at chest-4 (18:16:16, 10053), in the hall again. The
recording was not the trigger.

**sense130 (game on the SSD) dropped too:** five times (18:36, 18:46, 19:02, 19:05, 19:18), in the
waves and once in the opening cutscene. It is not the disk. The host's log at the first drop shows
the client's messages arriving until 18:36:24 and the host's writes stalling after 18:36:18. The
client had stopped reading the activity-host socket. At 18:36:39, 20 s after its last receive, it
declared "Lost connection to activity host" and closed it, while its own sent count kept rising.

**A socket diagnostic in the emulator** (fork branch `d1-net-diag`, `D1_NET_DIAG=1`). It logs every 2 s
per socket: receive calls, bytes, empty receives, sends, and the bytes Windows holds unread.
`tools/ps4_setup/d1-net-diag.py` reads it.
- The first build broke sign-in (sense131: `error.lost_bap_connection`). Its note ran between a failed
  `recv` and the caller's `WSAGetLastError()`, turning a non-blocking WSAEWOULDBLOCK into another
  error. The second build saves and restores that error.
- **sense132 (rebuilt emulator, diagnostic on):**
  - no drop through the waves;
  - the Divide reached at about 20:25 on its first connection;
  - two drought reports, the longest 10 s;
  - the game polled each live socket about 30 times a second, and no stall interval was flagged.

  - It played the whole mission (waves, Divide, hangar, outro, CONTINUE) on one connection.
- **sense133 (same binary, diagnostic off):** no drop and no drought reports through the waves to
  junction-west.

**Conclusion: the old emulator binary was the cause.** The binary deployed on 31 Oct (02:39) dropped
the session again and again from sense125 on. A rebuild of the fork's own source (branch `d1`, now
including the diagnostic, which is off unless `D1_NET_DIAG=1`) does not, with or without the
diagnostic.
- What exactly differed is not known. The old build was made 39 minutes after the last code commit
  it should contain, possibly from an uncommitted working tree.
- It is kept as `shadPS4.exe.before-net-diag`.
- Rule from this: deploy the emulator only as built from a committed fork revision, and record that
  revision.
